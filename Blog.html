I usually only write for myself, but circumstances have changed.

November 22, 2026

In these tumultuous times, my lodestar has been to protect things precious to me. What particular aspects of my particular life would I like to share? To preserve for the future?

I am so grateful I have had the opportunity to do mathematical research. I feel I have developed an aesthetic sense for something that cannot be directly seen, heard, or felt. Paradoxically, I sometimes ``feel'' a proof or ``see'' it in my head. These are only pale projections my mind creates in order to grasp an abstract object, but piecing together these projections gives rise to my personal vision of the abstraction. At the end of my PhD, I thought I was finally developing my own taste in mathematics.

Another miracle: sometimes it is clear an author holds the same projections as I do in their head. We may live in different continents or eras. We may never even meet; they might have even called me a slur if we ever had. Yet we have this connection of having constructed the same abstraction from its projection onto a page.

My undergraduate years were troubled; the exact content is not important here. But I came away with a single primary principle: my purpose must be the careful tending of the flourishing of others. The experiences described above are a special kind of flourishing that I would like to preserve. As one of the few privileged (and I mean that in multiple senses) to have had those experiences, I feel a compulsion to do what I can to protect and impart them.

I think it is now undeniable that LLMs will be used in math research. I do not claim that LLM usage categorically denies the kind of flourishing I've described, but I am very skeptical. I cannot see any way to develop this aesthetic sensibility without intense, deliberate effort impeded by mistakes. My understanding came in many thin layers, shaped by intertwining forces of chance and personal inclination. I worry that my budding taste will be crushed by the unrelenting waves of LLM usage in the coming years as we all imbibe the same flavors of math from the same handful of models. I anticipate the despair of attempting to construct an abstraction from words on a page with no comprehensible intention from the other side, only a machine. I would gladly welcome anyone who can show me that the particular varieties of joy I hold so dearly are still accessible with the use of LLMs.

I acknowledge that my inability to experience the joy of mathematical research with LLMs may simply mean that I am not suited for this new era of mathematics. So be it.
